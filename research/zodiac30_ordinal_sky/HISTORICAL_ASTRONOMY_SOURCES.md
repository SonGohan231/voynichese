# Historical astronomy sources for 1404–1430

## Primary and near-primary evidence

### Triple Jupiter–Saturn conjunction of 1425

A late-medieval horoscope survives for the triple conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn in 1425. The event was computed in the Parisian Alfonsine Tables tradition and interpreted astrologically by S. Belle. The three closest conjunctions occurred in tropical Scorpio, approximately at 17°, 16° and 13° Scorpio.

Sources:

- José Chabás and Bernard R. Goldstein, discussion of the horoscope for the 1425 triple conjunction and the Parisian Alfonsine Tables tradition.
- Rice University, description of a manuscript horoscope for the 1425 triple conjunction.

This is historically admissible evidence that a technically trained fifteenth-century astrologer could calculate, draw and interpret this particular event.

### Solar eclipses in the manuscript date window

NASA eclipse catalogues document major eclipses in the interval, including:

- 1406-06-16 — total solar eclipse;
- 1409-04-15 — annular solar eclipse visible in parts of Europe;
- 1415-06-07 — total solar eclipse crossing Europe;
- 1424-06-26 — total solar eclipse, near-total over parts of central Europe.

The exact local appearance depends on provenance, which is unknown. Therefore eclipse visibility is computed at several reference locations rather than selecting one location after the fact.

### Fixed stars

Across 1404–1430 general precession shifts the stellar coordinate frame by only about 0.36°. This is far too small to yield a visually unique year-specific fixed-star pattern at manuscript scale. Fixed stars remain useful as a background for moving planets, conjunctions, eclipses and occultations.

## Computation source

The generated ephemeris uses Swiss Ephemeris with the built-in Moshier planetary model and the Julian calendar. It computes geocentric tropical ecliptic longitude for the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn.

## Interpretation rule

Historical availability and numerical coincidence are not evidence of manuscript encoding. A candidate event must predict a page-specific feature that was not selected after inspection.
