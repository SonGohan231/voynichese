# Deferred candidate: SCORPIO-1425-TRIPLE-CONJUNCTION-STRUCTURE-01

## Observation external to the manuscript

In 1425 Jupiter and Saturn formed a triple conjunction in tropical Scorpio. Approximate conjunction longitudes were 17°, 16° and 13° Scorpio. The event was calculable with late-medieval astronomical tables and a horoscope for it survives.

## Single falsifiable claim

If f73r was designed to encode this event rather than a generic Scorpio month/sign, independently numbered positions corresponding to 13, 16 and 17 Scorpio should carry a page-specific, preregistered anomaly that is absent at the corresponding positions of the other signs.

## Allowed target features

Before viewing the target positions, exactly one feature family must be selected from the neutral atlas:

- exceptional radial connection;
- exceptional central-to-peripheral connection;
- paired or merged star marker;
- three-item local cluster;
- label feature previously defined without reference to 1425.

It is invalid to inspect positions 13/16/17 and then choose whichever feature happens to occur there.

## Required controls

- every triplet of three positions having the same spacing pattern as 13/16/17;
- all circular rotations of the triplet;
- corresponding positions in the other complete signs;
- other close planetary conjunctions in 1404–1430;
- full cost for choosing year, event, sign, orientation and ordinal zero.

## PASS

PASS requires:

- one frozen orientation and ordinal zero derived independently of 1425;
- enrichment at 13/16/17 with permutation `p <= 0.01`;
- at least 10 bits positive MDL after charging for event selection;
- no equal or stronger decoy triplet;
- no dependence on one ambiguous object.

## Current status

`DEFERRED_BLOCKED_BY_VISUAL_ORDINAL_MAP`.

The candidate is historically possible and unusually specific, but presently weak. f73r is not known to contain a pair or triple planetary symbol at the target degrees, and the ordinal zero of the panel is not certified. The candidate must not influence the current label-order experiment.
